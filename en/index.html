From signal to application

Three outcomes at three timescales
from continuous waveforms

After phase picking, continuous waveforms follow two paths, one focused on speed and one on completeness. They produce three outcomes at different timescales, each serving a different application.

Continuous waveform

Raw ground-motion signals transmitted continuously from stations, 7×24.

Phase picking

Deep learning identifies P- and S-wave arrival times in noisy data, the basis for every later decision.

Earthquake early warning

Arrivals from a few stations are enough to estimate the impact without waiting for a full catalog, providing seconds of response time before strong shaking arrives.

Near-real-time

Earthquake catalog

Arrivals from multiple stations are combined to determine each event's location, magnitude, and origin time. The official catalog is produced by analysts through manual picking and location in the workstation, which also builds up labeled data for AI training.

Analyst workstation

Earthquake Catalog Workstation

An earthquake analysis platform centered on manual analysis, developed with Taiwan's Central Weather Administration. As analysts pick phases, the system relocates the event in real time, evaluates location quality, and identifies stations that still require picks, keeping the analysis workflow continuous. Analysts can concentrate on waveform interpretation, and confirmed results are exported as the official catalog and used as labeled data for training seismic AI.
