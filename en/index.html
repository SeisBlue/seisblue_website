From signal to application

Three outcomes at three timescales
from continuous waveforms

After phase picking, continuous waveforms follow two paths, one focused on speed and one on completeness. They produce three outcomes at different timescales, each serving a different application.

Continuous waveform

Raw ground-motion signals transmitted continuously from stations, 7×24.

→
Seconds

Phase picking

Deep learning identifies P- and S-wave arrival times in noisy data, the basis for every later decision.

Seconds

Earthquake early warning

Arrivals from a few stations are enough to estimate the impact without waiting for a full catalog, providing seconds of response time before strong shaking arrives.

Near-real-time

Earthquake catalog

Arrivals from multiple stations are combined to determine each event's location, magnitude, and origin time.

→
Long-term

Background & anomaly

The accumulated catalog establishes background seismicity, and deviations from it indicate subsurface changes that merit further investigation.

In the field

Seconds

Automated protection for critical facilities

Those seconds feed into the automated protection or shutdown systems of critical infrastructure, so the alert triggers a response directly.

Near-real-time

A foundation for monitoring & response

Monitoring networks and disaster-response agencies receive stable, reproducible near-real-time earthquake information as a shared basis for assessment and public reports.

Long-term

Joint development of anomaly detection

SeisBlue has a strong research foundation in deep learning and aims to develop background and anomaly detection methods jointly, starting from partners' data and applications.

Method Research

BlueDisc · S-wave detection method

BlueDisc originates from Chun-Ming Huang's years of research on phase picking. Targeting the long-standing S-wave amplitude suppression problem in deep-learning phase pickers, the work dissects the role of the loss function in training and why the data fails to converge, and validates the findings through a shape-then-align training strategy with a conditional GAN proof of concept, raising effective S-phase detections by 64% on standard benchmarks. Source at github.com/SeisBlue/BlueDisc. For a non-technical explanation, see the article Why Does AI Keep Missing S-Waves? →

Huang, C.-M., Chang, L.-H., Chang, I.-H., Lee, A.-S., & Kuo-Chen, H. (2025). Recovering Sub-threshold S-wave Arrivals in Deep Learning Phase Pickers via Shape-Aware Loss. arXiv:2511.06731. arxiv.org/abs/2511.06731

Training Infrastructure

SeisBlue Platform · seismic data framework

SeisBlue is a deep-learning seismic data processing framework developed and open-sourced by Chun-Ming Huang. The platform packages the full pipeline, from waveform ingestion through phase picking to catalog output, into a service that runs reliably in production, adopted by multiple peer-reviewed studies as a core processing tool. Source at github.com/SeisBlue/SeisBlue.

Huang, C.-M., Chang, L.-H., Kuo-Chen, H., & Zhuang, Y. (2023). SeisBlue: a deep-learning data processing platform for seismology. EGU General Assembly 2023, EGU23-13927. doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu23-13927

Production Deployment

TT-SAM · real-time earthquake early warning

TT-SAM (Taiwan Transformer Shaking Alert Model) is an earthquake early warning system. Its architecture references the TEAM framework by Münchmeyer et al. (2020); Chun-Ming Huang led the AI engineering core, including training environment setup, pipeline design, and production deployment. The real-time pipeline runs on the Earthworm real-time data stream.

Chen, Y.-H., Chan, C.-H., Chang, C.-C., & Ma, K.-F. (2026). A Deep Learning Framework for Peak Ground Velocity Prediction Using Multi-Station Velocity Waveforms: The Taiwan Transformer Shaking Alert Model (TT-SAM). Journal of Geophysical Research: Machine Learning and Computation. doi.org/10.1029/2025JH001005
